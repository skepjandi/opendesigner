## What videos are for here

App Store and Google Play app previews, promo and changelog clips, web hero loops, short social cuts of a feature. Short, mostly screen captures and motion graphics, rarely a multi-camera edit. That shapes scope: a precise, scriptable timeline with strong title/overlay tooling beats a full colorist.

## Built: video v1

- **Model.** `Document.videos`: `Video {id, name, fps (1-60), width, height (even, 16-4096), clips}` and `Clip {id, frame, footage, duration_ms (100-60,000), transition {kind, duration_ms, ease}, motion {scale_from, scale_to, ease}, speed (1), speed_ramp ([]), grade (none)}`. Time is whole milliseconds, snapped to the video's frame grid (see **Time on the frame grid** below). A transition overlaps the end of the clip before it, so a 500 ms fade makes the video 500 ms shorter than its clips; a video is at most 10 minutes and 500 clips. Stored as `videos/<id>.json` (listed in `od.toml`), like pages.
- **Footage and overlays.** `Clip.footage {file: media/<name>.mp4|mov|m4v, in_ms}` instead of `frame` (exactly one); `Video.overlays [{id (o_..), frame, start_ms, duration_ms, fade_ms, enter, exit, ease}]` drawn over the clips, entering and leaving (at most half the duration each). Operations `add_overlay`, `remove_overlay`, `set_overlay`, and `set_clip` source. Footage files enter only through the user's import (`Workspace::import_footage`, sniffed by the `ftyp`/`moov` box, 8 GB cap, never overwriting, listed apart from images so an image node cannot name a video); `list_footage` shows them to the agent.
- **Operations** (undoable, attributed, checkpointed like any edit): `create_video`, `delete_video`, `set_video` (name, fps, size, **audio**), `add_clip`, `remove_clip`, `move_clip`, `set_clip` (duration_ms, frame, source, transition, motion, **speed**, **speed_ramp**, **grade**). `restore_video` and `restore_clip` are the history's own and are refused from front ends. A frame that a clip shows cannot be deleted until the clip is removed.
- **Rendering.** Each clip's frame is drawn by `render_frame` once at the scale the output needs (scaled to cover the video and cropped in the middle), then drawn with the zoom, offset and opacity of that moment: fade blends, slides push the old clip out, the wipes and the iris cut a mask out of the arriving clip (`od_render::transitions`), and a grade is applied to the picture the clip draws (`od_render::grade`).
- **Encoding.** `od_media` pipes raw RGBA frames to `ffmpeg` (`-f rawvideo ... -c:v <encoder> -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart`) and moves the finished `.part` file into `exports/` only when ffmpeg exits cleanly. It prefers an encoder the OS provides: `h264_videotoolbox` (macOS), `h264_mf` (Windows), then `libopenh264`, NVENC, QSV, AMF, VAAPI, and GPL `libx264` last. It looks for `ffmpeg` in `OPENDESIGNER_FFMPEG`, next to the app (and in a bundle's `Resources`), then `PATH`; without one the error says what to install. Release build on an M2 Pro: 5.4 s of 886x1920 in 7.8 s.
- **Store rules.** `od_store::video` presets validate size, length, frame rate (before any encoding) and file size (after); a failure writes nothing and names the rule, the page and the day it was read. Not checked: H.264 profile/level, the 10-12 Mbps target, the audio track (no audio rule source is recorded in `od_store::video`, so none was added).

- **Motion.** Eases (`linear`, `ease_in`, `ease_out`, `ease_in_out` (default), `back` with a small overshoot, `spring` with a damped overshoot) shape every transition, every clip zoom and every overlay entrance. Transitions: `cut`, `fade`, `slide_left|right|up|down`, `dip` (through black), `zoom_in`. An overlay has `enter` and `exit` (`none`, `fade` (default), `slide_up|down|left|right`, `pop`, `zoom`), each taking `fade_ms`, and an `ease`. Pose per moment: offset, scale and opacity, so an entrance is the overlay's own frame moved or scaled, not a new frame.
- **Keyframes.** `Video.animations [{node, prop, keys [{at_ms, value, ease}]}]`: any number of a node (`x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `opacity`, `corner_radius`, and on a 3D device its turn, tilt, roll, zoom, lens, light, shadow, glare and screen scroll) moves over the video's time. The operation `animate` replaces the keys of one property (an empty list removes them); the value holds before the first key and after the last. `Document::at_time` gives the document as it looks at a moment, and the renderer draws a frame that holds an animated node again for every moment it moves. Named moves (`spin`, `turntable`, `swing`, `flip_in`, `hero_reveal`, `rise_in`, `drop_in`, `float`, `push_in`, `pull_back`, `orbit`, `scroll_screen`, `fade_in`, `fade_out`, `slide_in_left`, `slide_in_right`) are written as ordinary keyframes measured from the node's designed pose: the MCP tool `animate_preset` and the CLI `animate` expand them, so a move is data the user can edit. `Video.motion_blur` (2 to 16 pictures) blends the moments across a half-frame shutter while something moves; a cut is never blurred across. Blocks keyframe the same way: `add_block` writes the same `animate` operations for the parts it makes, through `od_core::animation::preset_ops` for the named moves and plain keys for what they do not cover.
- **Motion.** Eases (`linear`, `ease_in`, `ease_out`, `ease_in_out` (default), `back` with a small overshoot, `spring` with a damped overshoot) shape every transition, every clip zoom and every overlay entrance. Transitions: `cut`, `fade`, `slide_left|right|up|down` (the new clip pushes the old one out), `dip` (through black), `zoom_in`, `wipe_left|right|up|down` (an edge uncovers the new clip), `iris` (a circle opens from the middle), `flash` (through white), `blur_dissolve` (both clips blur while the new one fades in) and `glitch_slice` (the picture breaks into seeded horizontal slices that slide sideways). All of them are drawn on the CPU and are the same every time they are drawn. An overlay has `enter` and `exit` (`none`, `fade` (default), `slide_up|down|left|right`, `pop`, `zoom`), each taking `fade_ms`, and an `ease`. Pose per moment: offset, scale and opacity, so an entrance is the overlay's own frame moved or scaled, not a new frame.
- **Keyframes.** `Video.animations [{node, prop, keys [{at_ms, value, ease}]}]`: any number of a node (`x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `opacity`, `corner_radius`, and on a 3D device its turn, tilt, roll, zoom, lens, light, shadow, glare and screen scroll, and on an image node `grade_exposure`, `grade_contrast`, `grade_saturation`, `grade_temperature`, `grade_tint`, `grade_vignette`, `grade_blur`) moves over the video's time. The operation `animate` replaces the keys of one property (an empty list removes them); the value holds before the first key and after the last. `Document::at_time` gives the document as it looks at a moment, and the renderer draws a frame that holds an animated node again for every moment it moves. Named moves (`spin`, `turntable`, `swing`, `flip_in`, `hero_reveal`, `rise_in`, `drop_in`, `float`, `push_in`, `pull_back`, `orbit`, `scroll_screen`, `fade_in`, `fade_out`, `slide_in_left`, `slide_in_right`) are written as ordinary keyframes measured from the node's designed pose: the MCP tool `animate_preset` and the CLI `animate` expand them, so a move is data the user can edit. `Video.motion_blur` (2 to 16 pictures) blends the moments across a half-frame shutter while something moves; a cut is never blurred across.
- **3D devices.** A `device3d` node in a frame is a phone, tablet, laptop or monitor drawn in perspective inside its own box (`od_render::three_d`, a CPU rasterizer with a depth buffer, supersampled anti-aliasing, a studio environment and a soft shadow). The screen takes the node's `image` (a capture) or a recording (`screen_footage`), which plays while the frame is shown. The device is turned, tilted and lit with plain properties, so the agent edits it like any other node and a video animates it with the same keyframes ([Document Format](./document-format.md#3d-devices)).
- **Sequence view.** `list_videos` returns `story`: one sentence per clip (when it starts, how it arrives and over how long, what it shows, its zoom and ease, when it ends) and per overlay (start, enter, exit, end). The window's Video tab draws the same as an editor timeline (`VideoPanel.qml`, tracks in `VideoTimeline.qml`): a transport bar (start, frame back, play/pause, frame forward, end, loop, timecode `mm:ss:ff`, zoom out/in, fit), the preview above it, a ruler whose ticks adapt to the zoom, and labelled tracks (Video, Overlays, **Sound with one row per track, each with its waveform, and Captions with a block per cue when there are any**; the next sentences describe the original three) whose headers stay put while the tracks scroll sideways: **Video** (clips as rounded blocks, footage and design frames coloured apart, with a strip of pictures taken from the cached preview once it is drawn; the stretch each transition shares with the clip before it is drawn as a marked overlap), **Overlays** (overlapping overlays stack on lanes) and **Sound** (the track from its start; there is no waveform because the app holds no audio data, and the file's own length is unknown, so the bar runs to the end of the video). A playhead crosses every track; click or drag the ruler or any track to scrub, Ctrl/Cmd + wheel or pinch to zoom around the pointer, wheel to scroll. The view fits the whole video until you zoom by hand. Picking a clip, overlay or the sound shows its properties in the Details tab (the Story tab keeps the sentences). Keys: Space, Left/Right (one frame), Home/End. Preview progress, an out-of-date preview and errors are shown over the picture; with no video the panel explains how to get one.
- **Sound track (the older one).** `Video.audio {file: media/<name>.mp3|wav|m4a|aac|ogg, start_ms (0-600,000), gain (0-2, 1 by default), fade_in_ms, fade_out_ms}`. It is set with `set_video` (`prop {name: "audio", value: {...}}`; `null` takes it away), so it is an attributed, undoable, checkpointed edit like any other, and it is stored with the video in `videos/<id>.json`. The file must be a plain name in the project's `media/` folder (the same rule an image node follows). Tracks never make the video longer: they are cut at the end. It stays valid and is read as the first of the tracks below (`Video::sound_tracks()`, id `audio`, role music).

## Transform, loops and camera

Nodes turn, scale, blur, cast shadows and clip, tracks repeat, and a video has a camera ([Document Format](./document-format.md#transform-and-effects)). Eases added: `ease_in_out_cubic` (the curve of `ease_in_out`), `ease_out_back` (the curve of `back`), `elastic` and `bounce`. The renderer treats a frame as live while a camera animation or a repeating track is under way (`VideoRenderer::signature`, `moving_at`), so an export redraws it for every moment and reuses a picture only while nothing moves; `Animation::value_at` folds a repeating track's time into its cycle, so frames are a pure function of the time and a parallel export is unchanged. The camera is applied to frames shown by clips, not to overlays.

## Scene motion

> **Status: Built** (`crates/agent/src/scene_motion.rs`, `scene_tool.rs`, `sequence.rs`, 2026-11-28). `animate_preset` takes named moves that work on any node (not only 3D devices) and on the video's camera. They are pure functions from the pose a node has where the move starts to ordinary keyframes, so a move can be read, edited and undone like any other `animate`: one tool call is one undoable step, keys inside the move's time range replace the old ones and the rest are kept, so moves chain. The same builders give the scenery blocks their loops. `sequence` places several moves against each other with a position grammar, and `spring_to` sends one property to a value on a spring.

Arguments: `video`, `node` (not for camera moves), `preset`, `start_ms`, `duration_ms` (a scene move has a default; the older moves need it), `ease`, `intensity` (the older `strength`, 0.1 to 4), `repeat` (`once|loop|pingpong`), `seed`, and per move `direction`, `amount`, `center_x`/`center_y`/`radius`, `path`/`orient`, `of`/`nodes`/`step_ms`/`from`, and a `stagger` object.

| Move | What it writes |
|------|----------------|
| `float` | y and x sine drift (3 and 1.2 percent of the node's size), loops |
| `bob` | y (6 percent) and a little rotation, loops |
| `breathe` | scale_y up and scale_x down by 3.5 and 1.75 percent, loops |
| `pulse` | one beat of scale (up to +10 percent), loops |
| `spin` | rotation, `intensity` turns, linear, loops (ends a turn on, which looks the same) |
| `wobble` | two small rotation swings and a jelly squash per cycle, loops |
| `swing` | rotation 14 degrees either way, loops |
| `shake` | x and rotation that die away (decaying sine), once |
| `pop_in` | scale 0 to 1 with `ease_out_back` (about 10 percent overshoot) and a quick fade in |
| `bounce_in` | y from above (0.45 of the stage height) with `bounce`, plus a fade |
| `elastic_in` | scale 0 to 1 with `elastic` |
| `slide_in_left\|right\|up\|down` | x or y from a distance, 6 percent past home, back; plus a fade |
| `fade_up` | opacity 0 to 1 while rising |
| `zoom_in` | scale from 0.6 to 1 with a fade |
| `draw_on` | `trim_end` 0 to 1 (the node needs a stroke) |
| `orbit` | x and y around `center_x`/`center_y` (default a point one radius to the left), sampled about every 33 ms, loops |
| `animate_along_path` | x and y (and with `orient` rotation) sampled from a `path` node's flattened curve at equal arc length, shaped by `ease`; a two-point path with a plain ease is two keys |
| `stagger` | any other move on every id of `nodes`, `step_ms` apart, `from` first, last, center, edges (both ends together, then inward) or random (seeded). With `columns` the nodes are a grid read row by row and each is delayed by its distance from the start cell: `start_index` (an index into `nodes`) or the cell `from` names (first is top-left, last bottom-right, center the middle, edges the outer ring first); `metric` is `manhattan` (a diamond wave), `euclid` (round, the default) or `chebyshev`; one unit of distance is `step_ms`. A `stagger` object adds value staggers, an eased or reversed distribution, a seeded jitter and real node centres (see [Stagger v2](#stagger-v2)) (`scene_motion/stagger.rs`) |
| `camera_push_in`, `camera_pull_back` | `camera_zoom` 20 percent (pull back from rest starts 20 percent in) |
| `camera_pan` | `camera_x` or `camera_y` by `amount` (default an eighth of the video size) toward `direction` |
| `camera_drift` | slow looping `camera_x`, `camera_y`, `camera_rotation` |
| `camera_shake` | decaying `camera_x`, `camera_y`, `camera_rotation` |
| `camera_whip` | a fast pan with `ease_in_out_cubic` and a tilt |
| `camera_burst` | a camera kick along `direction` by `amount` (default a sixth of the stage, scaled by `intensity`): `camera_x` or `camera_y` keys from the start to the new place with `power4_out`, so the speed peaks on the first frame and fades over nine frames of the video, then a key holds it until the move's end (default 600 ms) (`scene_motion/burst.rs`) |

### Sequencing: the position grammar

`sequence` (`crates/agent/src/sequence.rs`) places a list of moves against each other in one turn and one undo step. Each item names a `preset`, the `node` or `nodes` it moves, and where it starts (`at`):

| `at` | meaning |
|---|---|
| a number (`250`) | that many milliseconds from the start of the video |
| nothing | after the longest step so far |
| `+=100`, `-=100`, `*=2` | that much more or less than the end of the timeline (`*=2` is half of it twice over) |
| `<` | the end of the step added last (0 on an empty timeline) |
| `<<` | the start of the step added last |
| `<+=50`, `<<-=100`, `<*=2` | that much more or less than the end or the start of the step before |
| `intro`, `intro+=200` | where a step named `intro` was set |

An item may name itself with `label` (later items place against it) and spread its own nodes with a `stagger` object. `fit_to_ms` scales the whole sequence to that length before anything is written. The result echoes the resolved table of `{label, node, preset, start_ms, end_ms}` and the labels. A label that does not exist yet, an empty `at` and a start before zero are refused: a typo must not quietly become a number.

### Stagger v2

`animate_preset` with `preset: stagger` (and every `sequence` item) take a `stagger` object (`crates/agent/src/scene_tool/distribution.rs`, `scene_motion/stagger.rs`):

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `each_ms` | the delay per unit of distance between neighbours (the older `step_ms`; default 120) |
| `range` | a value stagger: `[near, far]` spreads those two values over the distances, and each node's intensity is multiplied by its value (a scale that falls off from the middle) |
| `from` | `first`, `last`, `center`, `edges`, `random` (seeded), an index into the nodes, or `{"point": [fx, fy]}` as fractions across the group |
| `grid` | `[columns, rows]`: the nodes are a grid read row by row (the older `columns`) |
| `metric` | `euclid` (the default), `manhattan` or `chebyshev` |
| `axis` | `x` or `y`: only the distance along that axis counts, signed |
| `ease` | shapes the distribution (`out_*` front-loads the wave) |
| `reversed` | the farthest goes first |
| `start_ms` | added to every delay |
| `jitter_ms` | a seeded random change per node: one size, or `[size at the first, size at the last]` |
| `seed` | seeds the random order and the jitter (the move's own seed by default) |
| `spatial` | distances are measured between the nodes' real centres, in units of the nearest pair |

A delay that would fall below zero (an `axis` reading, or a jitter) shifts every delay so the earliest is 0. The older top-level arguments (`step_ms`, `from`, `columns`, `metric`, `start_index`) still work and are the base the `stagger` object is laid over.

### Springs

`spring_to` sends one property of a node (or of the video's camera) to a value on a spring: the keys run from where the property is now to the value, over the time the spring needs to come to rest (`settle_ms`), and the result says when it looks done (`perceived_ms`, the `visual_ms` asked for) and when it has really stopped, so the next beat can be placed after it. A tuned spring ease (`spring(visual_ms,bounce)`, or the presets `spring_snappy` 250/0, `spring_smooth` 400/0, `spring_playful` 450/0.3, `spring_bouncy` 500/0.5, `spring_heavy` 800/0.1) on an entrance stretches the move to the time the spring needs to rest; the result says by how much (`crates/core/src/ease.rs`).

From the command line: `opendesigner animate <project> --video <id> [--node <id>] --preset <move> [--start-ms --duration-ms --intensity --ease --repeat --seed --direction --amount --center-x --center-y --radius --path --orient --of --nodes a,b,c --step-ms --from --columns --metric --start-index]`.

Ambient loops are sampled sines (12 to 40 straight pieces a cycle, never closer than a frame), end exactly where they start, and take their start phase from `seed`; the keys of every move are put on the video's frame grid before they are merged with the keys a node already has. Following a path uses the node's parent coordinates and the path's offset in the tree, ignoring turned or scaled ancestors `[INFERENCE: not audited for rotated parents]`.

### Additive layers and implicit from

Status: **Built** (`crates/core/src/animation.rs`, `crates/agent/src/layering.rs`).

A track may be `"additive": true` (`Animation.additive`, left out of the file when false; format version 6). Its keys are **offsets**: at every moment the value is the node's value as the replacing track gives it (or, with none, the node's own value) plus the additive track's value, so a `bob` can sit on top of a travel. The rules:

- at most one replacing and one additive track per node and property (an `animate` with `additive: true` replaces the additive one, with `additive: false` the replacing one); the list stays sorted by node, property, replacing before additive, and that order is the order they are applied in, so the result is deterministic;
- the sum is held to the property's own range at the end (opacity never goes past 0 or 1); an offset key may be negative and no wider than the property's whole span (opacity -1 to 1);
- a colour (`fill_color`) cannot be additive and is refused; a property the node lacks (a 3D turn on a rectangle) is refused as it is for a replacing track; the camera props may be additive (a handheld shake over a push-in);
- a moving additive track counts as moving for the renderer's picture signature, the review tools and the scene gates (`Animation::moving_at` is the same function), and `Document::at_time`, `Video::camera_at` and `Video::animated_value` (the stack for one property at one moment) all compose through `Animation::compose`;
- it is written with `apply_ops` `animate` (`additive: true`) or `animate_preset` with `layer: "additive"`: the move's values become offsets from where it starts, so `float` laid over a `rise_in` bobs along the rise. `layer` is for the older moves (3D and `rise_in`, `drop_in`, `float`, `fade_in`...); a scene move refuses it. Undo and redo carry the flag in the operation, so they put the right track back.

`animate_to {video, node, prop, to, at_ms, duration_ms, ease?, keep_later?}` sends a property to a value from where it is: the first key is the value the node's replacing track gives at `at_ms` (the node's own value when it has none), so interrupting a move does not jump. The keys of the old track from `at_ms` on are replaced (`keep_later` keeps the ones after the new move ends); a non-linear segment that `at_ms` cuts is baked into 8 linear pieces so the old curve is followed up to the cut (a key exactly at `at_ms` keeps its own ease); a looping track is refused. The result is one ordinary `animate`, so it is attributed and undoable in one step, and reports `from` and `replaced_keys`. Example: keys `[(0, 0), (1000, 100)]` all linear and `animate_to` 300 from 400 ms for 500 ms give `[(0, 0), (400, 40), (900, 300)]`.

### Layout transitions

Status: **Built** (`crates/agent/src/layout_transition.rs`).

Record, edit, diff, as anime.js layout animation does it, over stable node ids. `snapshot_layout {frame, name?}` stores, per node inside the frame or group, its box in the frame (turn and scale included), its stored x, y, width, height, opacity, solid fill, visibility and parent, as a small plain JSON file in `<project>/layout-snapshots/<name>.json` (names `a-z 0-9 . _ -`, at most 48 characters, no link followed, at most 500 nodes and 256 KB a file, at most 24 snapshots: the oldest is dropped; it needs a project folder). The agent then edits the document, and `transition_layout {video, snapshot, at_ms, duration_ms?, ease?, stagger?}` diffs the record against the live document and writes the keys in one attributed, undoable turn:

- a node in both that moved (more than 0.01 units), resized (more than 1 unit, so a half-unit change writes no width or height track) or changed opacity or solid fill gets keys from its old value to its live one over `duration_ms` (default 350, ease default `ease_in_out`); before `at_ms` it shows the old value, after the move the live one, and the keys its tracks already had on those properties from `at_ms` on are replaced;
- a node that is new, or newly visible, fades in (opacity 0 to its own); one that moved to another parent starts where it was on the page (its old box less the new parent's box) `[INFERENCE: a turned or scaled parent is not accounted for]`;
- a node that was deleted cannot be animated and is reported as leaving with a note: keep it, animate its opacity to 0, and set `visible` false afterwards; a node that is now hidden is reported the same way;
- `stagger` takes the arguments of the scene-move stagger (`each_ms` default 40, `from`, `reversed`, `grid`, `jitter_ms`...), once over the nodes that move and once over those that enter, in document order;
- it echoes a table of what each node does, plus `moved`, `entering`, `leaving` and `unchanged`.

Not built: the swap-at-midpoint cross-fade for reflowed text, per-property `enter_from` and `leave_to` overrides, and carrying a node's rotation or scale.

### Paths and morphing

Status: **Built** (`crates/agent/src/path_motion.rs` and `path_motion/`, `crates/core/src/morph.rs`, 2026-11-29). Four tools, all written as ordinary keys (and, for a morph, the shapes a path node morphs through) in one attributed, undoable turn.

**Arcs.** `animate_arc {video, node, from?, to, at_ms, duration_ms, ease?, arc?, orient?}` moves a node on a quadratic Bezier instead of a straight line. `from` and `to` are the node's `x` and `y` (its top left; `[x, y]` or `{x, y}`; `from` defaults to where the node is at `at_ms`). The control point is `from + delta * peak + perpendicular * (strength * |delta|)`; `arc` is `{strength 0..1 (0.5), peak 0..1 (0.5), direction auto|cw|ccw}`. `auto` bulges **up** (toward the top of the screen), or to the **right** on a vertical move, whichever way the node travels, so a back-and-forth keeps one side; `cw` means the path sweeps clockwise on screen (it bulges to the left of the way it goes), `ccw` the other. The curve is sampled every ~33 ms (`ceil(duration / 33)` pieces, 2 to 480) onto the video's frame grid; `ease` shapes how far along the curve the node is at each moment (a spring or back ease overshoots along the curve's own continuation). With `orient`, a rotation track follows the tangent, added to the node's rotation, unwrapped so it never jumps by 360 degrees. Keys outside the move are kept, so moves chain. The same `arc` object on `animate_preset` bends a scene move that travels in x or y: the move's own ease and timing give the progress along the run (read from the axis that goes farther), a move in one axis (`slide_in_*`, `bounce_in`, `fade_up`) is bent sideways from the node's rest position and gains the other axis's track, and a move that does not travel (`pop_in`), a camera move, `animate_along_path` and a looping move refuse it. `orient` is not applied to preset moves.

**Percentage keyframes.** `animate_keyframes {video, node?, keyframes, at_ms?, duration_ms, ease?, allow_over?}` takes CSS-style `{"0%": {"x": 0, "y": 0, "ease": "ease_out"}, "40%": {...}, "100%": {...}}` (`from` and `to` also read) over any animatable properties (camera properties need no node; `fill_color` takes `#rrggbb`). Each property has its own keys, at `at_ms + percent * duration_ms`. The rule is the one CSS and anime.js use: a keyframe's `ease` shapes the move that **leaves** it, while an OpenDesigner key's ease shapes the move that **arrives**, so each key takes the ease of the keyframe before it (or the tool's `ease`, default `ease_in_out`); the last keyframe's ease is unused and the result says so. A property whose first keyframe is after 0% starts from its current value (its track's value at `at_ms`, else the node's own) and goes there over the time before it. Percentages past 100 need `allow_over`; two keyframes that land on one frame are refused, not merged. Vector: duration 1000, `{0%: x 0 ease out, 50%: x 100 ease in, 100%: x 200}` gives keys `(0, 0)`, `(500, 100, out)`, `(1000, 200, in)`.

**Text on a path.** `text_on_path {text_node, path_node, start_offset?, align?, mode?, reveal?}` flattens the path node's first contour (curves cut into pieces of at most 1/256 of its longer side) into the text node's parent's coordinates, takes each glyph's advance from the renderer's own layout (`od_render::text_lines`, the source `split_text` cuts from; spaces keep their width but are not nodes) and stands each glyph on the curve: its middle on the point `start + advance before + advance / 2` along the path, its baseline on the curve, rotated to the chord of the curve across its advance (rotation about the glyph's middle on the baseline, the pivot). `align` places the line `start`, `center` or `end` along the path and `start_offset` shifts it. A closed path wraps; on an open one a glyph that would fall off an end is left out and counted. The glyphs are text nodes in a group (`<name>.p0..`, reading order) with the original's font, size, weight, line height, fill and stroke; `mode` `replace` (default) removes the original and the group takes its name, `keep` hides it. `reveal {video, preset?, each_ms?, at_ms?, duration_ms?, ease?}` staggers a scene move (default `pop_in`, 40 ms apart) over the letters through the same stagger code as `animate_preset` (at most 60 letters). Limits: an ancestor's rotation or scale is not applied to the path; kerning between glyphs is the layout's, a ligature is one glyph; right-to-left rows are laid left to right `[INFERENCE: not tried]`.

**Shape morph.** `morph_to {video, node, to_path, at_ms, duration_ms, ease?, reset?}` turns a path node into another. anime.js `morphTo` resamples both paths by arc length into the same number of points and blends the digits; this does the same with the improvements the research listed. Both shapes are flattened into the node's box coordinates (the target translated by the difference of the two nodes' positions, so it lands where it is, scale and turn of ancestors ignored); the point count is `max(ceil(longer length * 0.33), 16)` capped at 256 (a 100 unit line and a 50 unit line give 33); an open outline is cut with the first and last points at its ends, a ring into `n` points a `1/n` of its length apart; two rings get matching windings (the target is reversed when the signed areas differ) and the start index that minimises the summed squared distance (smallest on ties); a ring and an open line both become open lines. Contours are paired largest first; one with no partner shrinks to (or grows from) its middle. The result is stored on the node as `path_morph` (see [Document format](./document-format.md#vector-shapes)) with a `morph_progress` track `0 -> 1`. The node keeps its own path data, so at rest and outside a video it draws its own curves; while a video plays, `Document::at_time` swaps the node's path for the blend as a polyline (3 decimals, view box the morph's box). The video renderer (preview and export), the review tools and the scene checks all draw through `Document::at_time`, so they see the same shape and no renderer code knows a shape is morphing. A second `morph_to` on the same node, starting at or after the first ends, adds a stop (progress 2) and a hold key; it keeps the first morph's contours and point counts and lines the new shape up with the last one (a target with more contours is refused). `reset` drops the morph and its progress keys and starts over from the node's own path. Chosen over the alternatives: a path `d` cannot be a keyed number, and swapping or cross-fading sampled path nodes cannot interpolate shape.

Not built: a fill or stroke that morphs along with the shape (animate `fill_color` separately), a morph that follows later edits of the node's path (run `morph_to` again with `reset`), curves refit through the blended points (the shape is a polyline of at most 256 points a contour, smooth at 0.33 points per unit), a speed or easing per contour, and extrapolation past a stop (a spring ease on `morph_progress` is held to the first and last stop). Not verified: how convincing morphs between very different shapes (a star into a heart) look, and 3D or turned parents `[INFERENCE]`.

## Sound tracks

`Video.tracks`: up to 8 `SoundTrack`s besides `audio` (`crates/core/src/audio.rs`; fields and bounds in [Document Format](./document-format.md#sound-tracks-and-captions)): `role` (`music`, `voice`, `sfx`), `file`, `start_ms`, trim `in_ms`/`out_ms`, `gain`, `fade_in_ms`/`fade_out_ms`, `pan` and, on music, `duck {depth, attack_ms, release_ms}`. They change as a whole list through `set_video` `tracks`; `add_sound_track`, `set_sound_track` and `remove_sound_track` (MCP and `opendesigner sound add|set|remove`) compute the new list for one change. Files are plain names in `media/` with the audio extension rules and, for the tools, must exist.

- **Mixing.** After the picture is encoded, `od_media::add_audio` mixes everything in **one ffmpeg pass** (`crates/media/src/mix.rs`, `filter_graph`): each piece is `aresample`d to 48 kHz and `aformat`ed to stereo, then `atrim` (trim in and the played length), `volume`, `afade` in and out, `pan`, `adelay` to its start, and all are summed by `amix` (`normalize=0`) and padded with silence (`apad`) so the file ends where the picture does. The picture is copied (`-c:v copy`), not re-encoded; audio is AAC 192 kbit/s. Footage sound is mixed the same way at volume 1 and without fades, role `Footage`, never ducked. A track that starts after the video ends or whose trim is empty is left out and reported as a warning of `export_video`.
- **Ducking.** A music track with `duck` listens to the voice tracks through ffmpeg's `sidechaincompress`: the voice pieces are summed (`amix`), split (`asplit`) into the final mix and one side-chain per ducked music track, and each music piece goes through `sidechaincompress=threshold=0.03:ratio=<1..20>:attack=<ms>:release=<ms>:makeup=1`. `depth` 0 to 1 maps to ratio 1:1 to 20:1; the threshold is about -30 dBFS (linear 0.03). Chosen over a volume envelope computed in Rust because it follows the voice sample by sample with no analysis pass and the graph is a pure function of the pieces, so its construction is unit-tested without ffmpeg; the side-chain is `apad`ded because the compressor would otherwise end the music where the voice ends. Verified on this machine (ffmpeg 9.0.1, macOS) by `crates/media/tests/audio.rs`: with a 220 Hz bed and a 1.7 kHz voice, the low band under the voice is below half its level beside it, and returns after. With no voice track, ducking does nothing. Only `Voice` pieces drive it, not footage sound `[INFERENCE: a talking-head clip may deserve to duck music too; not built]`.

## Pauses and waveforms

- **`od_media::Ffmpeg::detect_pauses(file, noise_db, min_ms)`** runs `ffmpeg -af silencedetect` and reads `silence_start`/`silence_end` from the log (`analysis.rs`, `parse_pauses`); a pause still open at the end of the file ends there. Tools `detect_pauses` and `opendesigner sound pauses`.
- **Cutting.** `Video::pause_cut_ops(clip, pauses, keep_ms)` (`crates/core/src/pauses.rs`) turns a footage clip into the stretches of its file that are not pauses, keeping `keep_ms` of each pause at both ends: a `set_clip` on the first piece and `add_clip`s for the rest, in one transaction (one undo). A zoom (`motion`) is carried across the pieces. Pieces under 100 ms are dropped. Overlays, captions and tracks sit at absolute times and **do not move**; the tool says so. Tools `remove_pauses` and `opendesigner sound cut-pauses`. No new clip operation was needed.
- **Waveform.** `Ffmpeg::waveform(file, buckets)` decodes to 8 kHz mono 16-bit PCM (at most 30 minutes) and keeps the loudest sample of each of 50-2000 buckets (`peaks_from_samples`). Tool `get_waveform` and `opendesigner sound waveform`. The Video tab computes 1000 buckets per file once, caches them until the file changes (`crates/app/src/sound_rows.rs`) and publishes them in `video.sound_rows`, which `VideoTimeline.qml` draws on each sound row. Without ffmpeg a row has no waveform.
- **Speech-to-text is not built in.** Recognising speech needs a model; the transcript comes from the user's own speech MCP server, or from `transcribe`, which runs the user's own whisper program ([Agent Integration](./agent-integration.md#optional-programs-get_capabilities-transcribe-remove_background)). These pause tools work on loudness only; `transcribe` also reports gaps between words.

## Captions

`Video.captions {style, cues}` (`crates/core/src/captions.rs`): data, never script. Cues have `start_ms`, `end_ms`, `text` and optional word timings; the style has font family, `size_pct` of the video's height, weight, colour, `highlight` for the active word, `background` box, position, alignment, `max_lines` and `uppercase`, with presets `clean`, `bold_pop`, `karaoke`, `boxed`.

- **Importing** (`import_captions`, `opendesigner captions import`): SRT and WebVTT through one reader (BOM and CRLF accepted; tags, `{\an8}` and entities dropped; WebVTT inline `<00:00:01.500>` timestamps become word timings; a bad time is an error naming its line; cues are sorted and an overlap is cut where the next begins), and `words_json`, a list of `{text|word, start_ms|start (s), end_ms|end (s)}` grouped into cues at sentence ends, pauses of 700 ms and 8 words or 42 characters. The Whisper-style field names are an assumption from memory `[INFERENCE]`. `offset_ms` shifts; cues outside the video are dropped and counted. `captions_from_script` spreads a plain script over a time range **in proportion to word length: an estimate, not speech recognition**.
- **Rendering.** `od_render::captions::draw` (called last in `VideoRenderer::picture`, so captions are in the preview, `render_video_frame` and the export): the cue at that moment is wrapped to 90 % of the width with the same cosmic-text setup as every text, set smaller in up to six 8 % steps if it needs more lines than `max_lines` (then its first lines are shown), and each word is drawn separately so the active one (`Captions::active_word`: the word whose start has passed, held through a pause) takes the highlight colour. A cue without word timings is timed by estimate when a highlight is set. The picture signature includes the cue and the active word, so an export redraws only when the caption changes. Tests (`crates/render/tests/captions.rs`) compare pixels: the highlight colour exists and moves right between words, nothing is drawn outside a cue, a box darkens the line, and a long cue stays within `max_lines`.
- Not built: per-word animation (pop, bounce), karaoke wipes between words, fitting captions to platform UI zones, right-to-left or vertical text (cosmic-text shapes them, the line layout here is left to right `[INFERENCE: untested]`).

## Time on the frame grid

- **The rule.** A video shows frames, so a start, a length, an in point, a transition or a key that falls between two frames means nothing. Every time-valued input of a video operation is **snapped to the video's frame grid** when the operation is applied (`od_core::timebase`): the frame boundary nearest the value is taken, and a value outside its allowed range is left alone so the operation's own check refuses it with the rule it broke.
- **The arithmetic** is exact integer math, so nothing drifts however long the video is: frame `n` starts at `round(n * 1000 / fps)` ms, and the frame at or just before a moment `ms` is `floor(ms * fps / 1000)`. `od_core::timebase` is the only place that does this.
- **What is snapped.** A clip's `duration_ms`, a footage clip's `in_ms`, a transition's `duration_ms`, an overlay's `start_ms`, `duration_ms` and `fade_ms`, a speed ramp key's `at_ms` and an animation key's `at_ms`. The **recorded operation carries the snapped value**, so the receipt, the undo history and `list_videos` all show the number that was kept (at 24 fps, 1010 ms becomes 1000 and 1030 becomes 1042).
- **Old projects are left alone.** A project written before this rule keeps the times it has; the renderer reads each frame's time from the frame index, never by adding up floats, so an old project's picture does not move.
- **The renderer asks for a frame, not a moment.** `od_render::export` asks for frame `n` at `timebase::frame_ms(n, fps)`, which is the nearest boundary, so a clip that starts on the grid shows its first picture on exactly the frame that starts there.

## Speed and ramps

- **`speed`** (0.25 to 4, 1 is as recorded) is set with `set_clip` on a clip of footage. The clip's `duration_ms` is its length **on the timeline**; the clip plays `duration_ms * speed` of its file (`Clip::source_span_ms`). A clip at 2x that lasts 2000 ms plays 4000 ms of the file; at 0.5x it plays 1000 ms.
- **`speed_ramp`** (keys `{at_ms, rate, ease?}` in the clip's own time, at most 32) replaces the constant speed (a clip has one or the other) and eases the rate from key to key. The source time is the integral of that rate, computed a millisecond at a time (`od_core::speed::SpeedMap`), so the same clip always reads the same moment of the file.
- **The sound follows.** `od_media::retime` cuts the file's span into slices (one per 250 ms of timeline for a ramp) and stretches or squeezes each to its slice with `atempo`, which keeps the pitch; the result is mixed into the export like any other sound.
- **The export checks the file.** A clip that would read past the end of its file is refused with the numbers, before anything is encoded.

## Grading

- **`grade`** is a handful of plain numbers: `exposure` (-3 to 3 stops), `contrast`, `saturation`, `temperature` (cool to warm), `tint` (green to magenta) (all -1 to 1), `vignette` (0 to 1) and `blur` (0 to 50). It is set on a clip (`set_clip` `grade`) or on an image node (`set` `grade`), is stored as the numbers alone, and is undoable like any edit.
- **Named looks** (`warm`, `cool`, `mono`, `faded`, `punchy`) are only parameter sets: they are expanded into the numbers when the grade is read, so what is stored is always numbers. A grade may be given as `{"look": "warm", "exposure": 0.2}`; the numbers you give win over the look's.
- **The order is fixed** (`od_render::grade`): blur, exposure, temperature, tint, contrast, saturation, vignette. The colour maths runs on the stored values, as most editors' basic controls do, and is not done in linear light.
- **Blur** is three box passes (a Gaussian of the same sigma to within a few percent, at any radius), and its radius is in thousandths of the picture's shorter side, so a preview and the export blur by the same amount.
- **Keyframes.** The numbers of an image node's grade can be keyframed (`grade_exposure`, `grade_contrast`, `grade_saturation`, `grade_temperature`, `grade_tint`, `grade_vignette`, `grade_blur`), so a shot can pull focus or warm up over the clip.
- **Vector motion.** A stroke can be drawn on: `trim_end` (and `trim_start`, `stroke_width`) are keyframed numbers of any node with a stroke, so a line, a rounded rect or a path grows from nothing to its whole outline over the clip. `fill_color` keys take a color (`"value": "#rrggbb"`) and mix in sRGB, for shapes that change color. A frame is drawn from the document as it looks at that moment (`Document::at_time`), the same for the canvas, the preview and the export; a test pins `trim_end` 0 to 1 at the start, middle and end of a clip ([Document Format](./document-format.md#vector-shapes)).

## Time feel and loudness

- **More eases** (`Ease` in `crates/core/src/ease.rs`, snake_case names in every `ease` field and tool argument). `power2_in|out|in_out` and `power4_in|out|in_out` (t raised to 2 or 4; `power4_out` is the quick-start, long-settle curve), `expo_in|out|in_out`, `circ_in|out|in_out` and `sine_in|out|in_out` (the gentlest). The older names keep their curves. **Steps** hold a value instead of gliding: `steps_2`, `steps_3`, `steps_4`, `steps_6`, `steps_8` and `steps_12` split the stretch between two keys into that many equal pieces and show one evenly spaced pose in each (first piece the start value, last the end value), so a 1 s move with `steps_4` jumps four times, for the stepped feel of drawn animation. Tests pin 0 and 1 at the ends, monotonicity and the midpoints.
- **Tunable eases** (`od_core::ease`, 2026-11-22). An ease is still one string in a file or a tool argument, so every older project loads unchanged; the tunable forms are a name with numbers, kept in thousandths so `Ease` stays `Copy` and a file never holds a float that prints differently the next time it is saved (`Ease::to_string` writes the shortest form, `"spring(450,0.3)"`, and reads back the same). They are `cubic(x1,y1,x2,y2)` (the CSS `cubic-bezier`; x from 0 to 1, y from -2 to 3, solved by Newton's method with a bisection fallback), `back_in|back_out|back_in_out(overshoot)` (0 to 5; 1.70158 is the classic `back`), `elastic_out|elastic_in(amplitude,period)`, `power_in|power_out|power_in_out|power_out_in(exponent)` (1 to 8), `bounce_in`, `bounce_in_out`, `steps(n)` (2 to 64; the same poses as `steps_N`) and `anticipate` (pulls back a few percent first, then goes; the curve Motion calls `anticipate`, rebuilt from its Bezier). **Springs** are `spring(visual_ms,bounce)`: a damped spring with no starting speed whose move looks done after `visual_ms` (about 97 percent with no bounce) and that rings by `bounce` (0 none, 0.3 playful, 0.5 bouncy, negative slow and heavy, from -0.9 to 0.95). The damping ratio is `1 - bounce` (or `1 / (1 + bounce)` below zero) and the frequency is `2π / (1.2 · visual_ms)`; the position is the closed-form step response in its under-, critically and over-damped forms. A spring is the one ease with a length of its own: `Ease::apply_in(t, span_ms)` reads it over the stretch between two keys (`Animation::value_at` does), and what the spring has left at the end of a short stretch is spread over it so the value always lands on its key; `Ease::natural_ms` and `Spring::settle_ms` (the last moment it is more than 0.05 percent off) say how long a stretch must be for the swings to show (2 to 3 times `visual_ms`). The presets `spring_snappy` (250, 0), `spring_smooth` (400, 0), `spring_playful` (450, 0.3), `spring_bouncy` (500, 0.5) and `spring_heavy` (800, 0.1) are read-only names for the tuned form. The older `spring` keeps its fixed curve. Anything else is refused with the list of what is allowed (`od_core::ease::GRAMMAR`); `animate_preset`'s `ease` argument and `apply_ops animate` take all of them. Ideas from Anime.js (MIT) and Motion (MIT); the formulas are rebuilt from the published algorithms, no code is copied.
- **Hold frames ("on twos").** `Video.hold_frames` (1 to 6, default 1, left out of the file at 1; `set_video` prop `hold_frames`) makes every drawing last that many frames: the frames of a group of `hold_frames` all show the picture of the group's first frame (`Video::held_ms` maps a time to the start time of its group, using `timebase::frame_containing`, so a frame's own start time maps to its group's start and asking again changes nothing). `VideoRenderer::frame_at` and `signature` apply it first, so the editor's single frame, the preview and every worker of the parallel export agree and a held frame is a repeat the exporter and the preview cache already reuse; sound is not touched. With motion blur the blur is taken at the held moment. Pair it with `steps_*` eases for a hand-drawn rhythm.
- **Loudness.** `export_video` takes `loudness` (-14 social, -16 podcast, -23 broadcast; none by default, which leaves the level as mixed), `opendesigner export-video --loudness -14`. `od_media::loudness` runs ffmpeg's `loudnorm` on the final mix in **two passes**: the first measures the mix (`print_format=json`, read back by `Measured::parse`), the second applies `loudnorm=I=<target>:TP=-1.5:LRA=11:measured_I=...:linear=true` from that measurement, which lands closer than the single dynamic pass; if the first pass prints nothing usable the second falls back to the single-pass filter, and a silent mix is left alone. The filter sits between `amix` and the final `apad` (`mix::filter_graph_with`), so it sees exactly the exported sound. A video without sound ignores the option. The window has no video export options panel yet, so only the tool and the command line expose it. Tests build the filter strings and, when `ffmpeg` is installed, measure the exported file's integrated loudness for tones at several levels within 1.5 LU of each target.

## Parallel export

- **Frames are a pure function** of the document and the frame index, so they can be drawn in any order. `od_render::export` draws them on several threads (one per core the program may use, at most eight) and gives them to the encoder **in order** from a small reorder window, so memory stays bounded however long the video is.
- **Footage is the catch**: decoding is a stream, so each worker owns its own decoder and work is handed out in short runs of consecutive frames; a decoder is started (by seeking) at the start of a run. The run boundaries depend only on the video, never on the number of threads, so **any thread count writes the same file** (`crates/agent/tests/motion_export.rs`).
- **While nothing moves** the picture is the same: it is drawn once and delivered again.
- **The encoder may differ in a byte or two of its container from run to run** (VideoToolbox does), so exports are compared by the pictures they show, not by their bytes.

## Audio sources

A video's sound track is any file in `media/` named `.mp3`, `.wav`, `.m4a`, `.aac` or `.ogg`. Four ways to get one there, none of which needs the others:

- **`make_sound`** (`crates/agent/src/synth.rs`): the built-in sound maker. Without a `seed` each call takes a new random one (returned and kept in the sidecar), and a seed also moves the pitch along a major pentatonic and picks the tempo of a `beat` when none is given, so sounds differ between videos; seed 1 keeps the old defaults. Kinds `click`, `pop`, `tick`, `whoosh`, `swoosh_up`, `swoosh_down`, `riser`, `hit` (alias `impact`), `chime`, `drone` (alias `pad`; loops without a seam), `beat` (kick on every beat, hat between; `bpm` 40-240 and `bars`) and `stinger` (2 or 3 notes). Arguments `{kind, duration_ms?, seed?, pitch_hz?, bpm?, bars?, stereo?, name?}`. Pure Rust, no dependency: sines, noise, filters and envelopes, written as 16-bit PCM WAV at 44.1 kHz into `media/<name>.wav` with `media/<name>.synth.json` beside it (kind, seed, "synthesized, no third-party licence"). Every sound is normalized to a peak of 0.89 (about -1 dBFS), starts and ends at exactly zero after a short fade (2 ms for the drone, 8 ms otherwise), and is deterministic for a given kind, length, pitch and seed. It makes effects and simple loops, not music or speech.
- **`search_sounds` / `import_sound`**: Freesound, below. Needs the user's token; music beds are better from here.
- **`import_file` with an audio file** (`find_files` `kind: "audio"`): copies the user's own `.mp3 .wav .m4a .aac .ogg` into `media/`. The first bytes must match the extension (`ID3` or an MPEG frame for mp3, `RIFF…WAVE`, `ftyp` with an audio brand `M4A `/`M4B ` for m4a, an ADTS header for aac, `OggS`); a video container renamed `.m4a` is refused. Up to 512 MB, never overwritten, never modified.
- **A voice-over** from a speech MCP server the user added in the Connect tab (for example `minimax-mcp`), imported with `import_file` once it exists on disk.

`list_sounds` lists everything audio in `media/`: file, size, length (when ffmpeg is installed), `source` (`synthesized`, `freesound` or `imported`) and, for Freesound sounds, the attribution line. Any of them is set with `set_video` `audio`. The skill's *Sound* sections give the craft: a bed under a voice at gain 0.15 to 0.3, fades of 300 to 800 ms, hits on cuts, silence as a tool, never a silent video unless asked.

## Assets in a video

The bundled icons, generated pictures and pack art (`search_assets`, `make_asset`, `import_asset`; [Store Assets](./store-assets.md#asset-packs-icons-shapes-backgrounds-sounds)) are PNGs in `media/`. They reach a video through the same typed ops as any picture: an `image` node (`image: media/<file>`) in a design frame, and the frame is a clip or an overlay. Sounds from `import_asset` or `make_sound` are the track set with `set_video` `audio`. The `video` playbook tells the agent to take inventory (`list_media`, `list_footage`, `list_sounds`, `search_assets`) before building.

**One video per row.** `opendesigner export <project> --batch rows.json --video <id>` and the `batch_export` tool render a video once per row of template-variable values (a name, a headline, a language) on a scratch copy of the document, each through the normal `export_video` path and the same preset check, at most 20 rows a call ([Agent Integration](./agent-integration.md#template-variables-and-batch-export)). Per-language text overrides (`locales`) are a different mechanism: see [Document Format](./document-format.md#template-variables).

## Finding sounds: Freesound

The agent can put music and sound effects on a video. It finds them on [Freesound](https://freesound.org), a library of Creative Commons sounds, through its API v2 (`crates/agent/src/freesound.rs`).

- **The user's token.** Freesound asks every caller for a free API token, which the user gets at <https://freesound.org/apiv2/apply/> and pastes into the Connect tab. It is stored in the per-user config folder as `freesound/token` (readable by the user only) and sent only to freesound.org, in the query string where the API documents it. No agent can write it: the tools refuse with a message that says what to do ("no Freesound token: ask the user to add one in the Connect tab; they can get one at https://freesound.org/apiv2/apply/"). The token never goes into a project, an export or a log.
- **The tools.** `search_sounds {query, duration_max_ms?, page?}` lists sounds (id, name, length, license, author, a preview to listen to); `import_sound {id, name?}` downloads the preview mp3 into the project's `media/` folder and writes `media/<name>.freesound.json` beside it (id, title, author, license, license url, source url); `list_sounds` shows what is already imported. Nothing is overwritten: a free name is found instead.
- **The preview, not the original.** The file that comes down is Freesound's mp3 preview (about 128 kbit/s), because the original needs the user's own OAuth token. A video that needs the original recording has to be brought in by the user.
- **The license is the author's.** Freesound hosts sounds under many licenses (CC0, attribution, attribution noncommercial). The license travels with the sound: the tool's answer and the sidecar say which one it is, and the attribution line ("Title by Author, License, https://freesound.org/s/<id>/") is what the user has to show wherever the sound is used. The tool descriptions tell the agent this; the app's Video tab repeats it. We do not relicense anything.
- **The client.** `crates/agent/src/http.rs` is a small HTTP/1.1 client written for this: one `GET`, one answer, a connect and a read timeout, a cap on the answer's size, and a redirect that may only lead to the same site as the request that started it. The secure channel is `rustls` with the root certificates the operating system trusts. Nothing else in the app reaches the network.

### Preview in the Video tab

Play in the Video tab plays a **cached preview**, not a live render. When a video is shown (or chosen, or changed) a worker thread draws every picture of it from a detached copy of the project (`od_agent::video_preview`, the same copy `export_video` uses), one picture after the other so footage is decoded as a stream. The window shows "Preparing preview N%"; Play starts once the first second is drawn and keeps going while the playhead stays behind the drawn pictures (it shows "Buffering" and waits when it catches up). The video loops at its end.

How the preview stays cheap:

- **Pictures are kept by what they were made from.** `od_agent::preview_key` gives every moment a key: the video's settings, the clips and overlays on screen with the design frames they show, the animations, the colour tokens and the project's media files (as the file stores them, so a field added to a clip later is in the key without touching the code). The renderer's own `signature` says when two moments of one video look alike; the key also says what they look like. After an edit only the pictures whose key changed are drawn again; the rest are taken from the preview being replaced (`Preview::reusable`).
- **A picture that holds still is drawn once** and shared (`VideoRenderer::signature`).
- **The size and the rate follow the machine.** `od_agent::preview_tier` tries the longest sides 360, 540, 720 and 960 px at 15 fps or the video's own rate, and takes the best tier whose pictures fit the memory cap and can be drawn in a share of the video's length. The first preview measures the machine (three pictures) and hands the estimate to the next one, so a fast machine gets a sharp, smooth preview and a slow one a small, cheap one.
- **Pictures are kept run-length coded**, not as PNG (`od_agent::preview_frames`): a flat design frame shrinks by more than ten times, and a picture that does not shrink (footage) stays raw. Qt reads the raw pixels through the same image provider the canvas uses.
- **The sound is mixed once.** The footage clips' own sound and the sound track are mixed into a WAV (`od_media::mix_wav`, the same pieces and the same graph the export mixes) and played by Qt Multimedia (`MediaPlayer` and `AudioOutput`). The mix is named by what went into it, so an edit that leaves the sound alone does not mix again.

Limits, stated in the panel too:

- **Sharp and smooth, within the cap:** the tier is chosen as above; the exported MP4 is full size at the video's rate.
- **With sound:** the mixed WAV is played; a video with no sound says so, and a machine without `ffmpeg` says the sound could not be mixed.
- **Bounded memory:** about 256 MiB of pictures; a picture shown more than once is counted once. At the cap the preview keeps what it has and the panel says so.
- **Repaired, not cancelled, on change:** any change to the project (the log grows: the user's edit, an agent's, undo) makes the cache stale; the new preview takes the pictures the edit left alone and draws the rest. Switching video or closing the panel cancels it too. While the cache is not there yet, scrubbing draws the one picture asked for on demand.
- **The playhead follows the sound.** Two clocks run, the wall clock and the sound player's position. `app::preview::step` decides each tick: the player's position is the reference and the playhead leans toward it by a tenth of a tick (so a player that reports its position in coarse steps does not make the picture step), and when the two are more than 150 ms apart (after a stall, a seek or a loop) the player is moved to the playhead, which is right.

## Timeline model (intended)

- A **composition** page has an ordered list of tracks (video, audio, overlay, text), each holding clips with `start`, `duration`, `source in/out`, transform, effects and keyframes. Clips are nodes with stable ids, so the operation set is the same family as design operations plus `trim`, `split`, `retime`, `ripple`.
- A composition is also a **frame**: a video page can embed design frames as overlay clips (a device frame around a screen recording, a headline card), rendered by `render`.
- Time is an integer count of ticks per second (rational, never float seconds) so edits do not drift.
- Preview decodes through `media` (or Qt Multimedia for plain playback, see below) to frames shown in the preview panel at the project frame rate; scrubbing prioritizes latency (nearest keyframe plus decode forward) over exactness, with an exact frame on pause.
- Export encodes H.264/HEVC/ProRes where the platform allows, plus VP9/AV1 where the LGPL build includes them; presets for App Store app previews and Play promo video sit beside the image presets ([Store Assets](./store-assets.md)), with their own validation (resolution, frame rate, duration, size limits) `[INFERENCE: rules to be collected from Apple and Google docs in Phase 5]`.

## Qt Multimedia and FFmpeg

Read from Qt 6.12's Qt Multimedia docs: it is LGPLv3 (or GPLv2) and its **default backend is built on FFmpeg** on every platform except WebAssembly and embedded Linux. The binary packages link FFmpeg (9.0.1 in the 6.12 docs) *dynamically*; an application either bundles those libraries or depends on the system's, and may deploy **its own FFmpeg build provided the major version matches the one Qt uses**. Qt's prebuilt FFmpeg includes only features under permissive licenses. Native backends (AVFoundation on macOS; Windows Media Foundation, deprecated since Qt 6.10) are kept with limited support, and new features land only in the FFmpeg backend. Qt's docs warn that codec and hardware-acceleration behavior differs per platform, and that H.264 and similar codecs may carry patent royalties the Qt license does not cover (a distribution decision for the Phase 7 release work).

What follows for us:

- **One FFmpeg in the process** ([invariant 10](../../AGENTS.md)). Our `media` crate and Qt's backend must share the same major version; if we ship our own FFmpeg build, Qt loads it too.
- **Use Qt Multimedia for what it is good at** (plain playback, audio output, `QVideoSink` access to decoded frames, `QVideoFrameInput` to feed frames in) and our `media` crate for what an editor needs (frame-accurate decode, export, thumbnails, waveforms). Where they overlap, pick one in the Phase 0 video spike and delete the other path.
- **Do not depend on the deprecated native backends.**

What the app does with it (built, 2026-11-20): the preview's sound is a WAV that `od_media` mixes with the user's own `ffmpeg` and Qt Multimedia plays; nothing of ours is decoded by Qt, so no backend is chosen by us and the app does not need Qt's FFmpeg libraries. `crates/app/build.rs` links `Multimedia` (LGPLv3, checked in [Qt Desktop](./qt-desktop.md#licensing)) and `scripts/package-macos.sh` refuses a bundle that carries Qt's FFmpeg libraries, which would be a GPL build.
